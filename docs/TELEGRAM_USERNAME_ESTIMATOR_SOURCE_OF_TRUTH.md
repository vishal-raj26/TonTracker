## Retrieval, semantics and ranking

D1 retrieves own history first (up to 100 rows per target, at most 50 targets), then cached semantic related names, global recent evidence and structural cohorts within a bounded overall result. The per-name window prevents a frequently sold name from crowding out another name's own history. This is bounded retrieval, not a guarantee of complete lifetime history.

Semantic knowledge supplies categories, entity/word relationships and related terms to retrieval and comparison. It is cached; no LLM generates the final numeric price. New knowledge records carry an observation timestamp. Historical evaluation rejects missing or future observation timestamps, avoiding contemporary semantic knowledge leaking backward.

Ranking combines semantic/lexical relationships, classified route and structure, recency and reliability. Exact history is retained; the best 80 non-exact candidates are considered. Outlier robustification downweights unusual observations. Explanations preserve selected reasons, dates, original and adjusted USD amounts, reliability, event/asset identity and source URL when present. The detail view shows a compact subset.

The ranking weights remain heuristics, not a learned economic-relevance ranker. Semantic relevance is incomplete, especially multilingual entities and exceptional premium names. Similar string structure alone is not proof of economic interchangeability.

## Valuation and historical-sale treatment

The comparable center is a weighted median in log-price space. Exact transactions are blended separately so they are not counted twice as both own evidence and generic comparables. Own history receives strong age-sensitive influence but no permanent minimum. The reported historical anchor uses the same market adjustment as the calculation.

Market movement uses same-username repeat transactions within the target route, excluding the target itself. Recent and earlier windows require at least eight independent pairs. A dispersed/no-direction sample returns a flat adjustment; otherwise the repeat-sale log ratio is shrunk and bounded. Sparse data does not fabricate a market index. These thresholds and decay parameters are conservative engineering choices, not empirically universal constants.

Without strong direct evidence the trained log-price ridge/cohort model contributes a geometric mixture, avoiding arithmetic premium-tail inflation. With no retained comparable, the learned model requires at least eight structural-cohort or twelve semantic-cohort observations, matching its existing cohort-blending support thresholds. Otherwise it returns unavailable with reason `unsupported-model-cohort`; runtime caches that terminal result for two minutes. This blocks unsupported extrapolation, but is not a fully validated premium out-of-distribution detector. With neither source, the estimator returns unavailable.

Training deduplicates economic events across provider IDs, selecting the strongest valid provenance once. Nonfinite reliability is rejected. Future or undated external semantic knowledge cannot enter historical fitting or calibration. Runtime honors explicit stored portfolio exclusions while retaining the indicative detail estimate.

Publication safety is shared by runtime and the username baseline writer: only finite positive estimated values with medium/high evidence confidence and no explicit exclusion can be portfolio-eligible. Low-confidence or indicative values retain their detail estimate/range but contribute zero to priced holdings, even if an older stored record claims eligibility. The legacy PostgreSQL worker additionally retains its existing two-own-sales requirement. This is a conservative presentation rule, not an empirically calibrated guarantee for medium/high bands; premium accuracy remains unresolved.

Frontend normalization preserves the indicative username detail state without including it in portfolio value. Stale/processing username estimates are cleared from that normalized view instead of resurfacing as current detail values. Actual normalization/renderer tests cover these paths. The v25 full suite passed272 tests; all5 focused detail/publication tests passed after the final frontend change. No new live-wallet/browser verification is claimed.

## Range and confidence

The learned model uses a chronological 80/20 holdout where enough training rows exist, fitting only strictly earlier rows and measuring held-out log residuals. Residual endpoints supply model ranges. Comparable quantiles and sparse-evidence expansion contribute to the final envelope. This is not a proven universal 80% confidence interval: final observed coverage varies materially by cohort and time window.

Evidence confidence combines observable reliability, recency, similarity, effective independent weight and log dispersion. Sparse own history is capped low. `confidenceBasis` exposes the factors and explicitly denies a probability interpretation. Learned-only confidence uses held-out support where available. Labels are evidence quality, not probability of selling at the displayed price.
